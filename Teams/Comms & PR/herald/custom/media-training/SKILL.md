# Media Training

## Introduction

This skill packages the standard media-training frameworks (T.J. Walker + Neil/Bassett
practitioner corpus + SPJ on-record standards + Poynter Institute institutional
materials) into herald's operational entry point for spokesperson preparation. When a
`media-relations` pitch lands and produces an interview — or when an inbound reporter
inquiry escalates to a live conversation — the spokesperson needs preparation. Without
it, even well-prepared executives freeze on hostile questions, drift from key messages,
or accidentally cross on-record boundaries.

Added per §2 roster expansion — not in the original catalog. The catalog covered
media-relations (pitching) + press-kit (canonical content) for herald; media-training
fills the obvious gap between "pitch landed → interview scheduled" and "interview
happens well."

## Purpose

Prevents four failure modes that show up when spokespeople go into interviews unprepared:

1. **Message drift.** Spokesperson enters with 8 things to say; reporter picks up on
   the 3rd and drives the whole interview around a tangential point. Coverage doesn't
   reflect the intended story. The 3-message-max discipline (Principle 1) prevents this.
2. **Freeze on hostile questions.** Reporter asks the difficult question (competitor
   comparison, past mistake, tough industry critique); spokesperson stammers, agrees
   with the framing implicitly, or lies. Bridging technique (Phase 3) and hostile-Q
   drill (Phase 4) train the response.
3. **On-record boundary breach.** Spokesperson says something in what they thought was
   off-record but was actually on-the-record; or references confidential information
   thinking the reporter wouldn't use it. SPJ-standard boundary clarity (Phase 5)
   prevents this.
4. **Fact error live.** Spokesperson misstates a company stat, product feature, or
   executive credential during the interview. Post-interview corrections become
   crisis-comms events (route to beacon). Dry-run rehearsal (Phase 6) catches these
   before they ship.

herald uses this skill whenever media-relations produces an interview that matters —
typically for founders, executives, or subject-matter experts speaking to reporters
covering something material to the business.

## Structure / Protocol

The media-training preparation sequence:

```
1. INTERVIEW SCOPE INTAKE
    Reporter + publication + topic + format (in-person / phone / video / written Q&A) +
    date + duration + on-record status (default: on-record unless negotiated otherwise).

2. MESSAGE MAP (3-messages MAX)
    3 key messages the spokesperson wants coverage to reflect. Each message has:
      a. The message itself (1 sentence)
      b. 2-3 proof points (data / example / third-party validation)
      c. Anticipated challenge to the message (hostile-Q anchor)
    Sourced from press-kit canonical library (single source of truth).

3. BRIDGING DRILL (from ANY question → to KEY message)
    ABC formula (Walker + Neil/Bassett):
      A: ACKNOWLEDGE the question briefly (never dismiss it)
      B: BRIDGE with a transition ("What's important here is..." / "The bigger point
         is..." / "Let me put this in context...")
      C: COMMUNICATE the key message
    Practice bridging from 5-10 anticipated questions to each of the 3 messages.

4. HOSTILE-Q DRILL
    Anticipated hostile questions (competitor comparison; past failure; industry
    critique; regulatory concern; personal-life question). For each: recognize the
    pattern (loaded premise? false dichotomy? gotcha frame?) → reframe (don't accept
    the premise) → bridge to a truthful key message.
    Rule: don't lie. Rule: don't accept a false premise. Rule: reframe honestly.

5. ON-RECORD BOUNDARY CLARITY (SPJ standards)
    Confirm status BEFORE the interview starts:
      - ON-RECORD (default): everything usable + attributable
      - OFF-RECORD: not usable in any form
      - BACKGROUND: usable, not attributable to spokesperson
      - DEEP BACKGROUND: usable, not attributable to org
    Off-record and background require EXPLICIT reporter agreement BEFORE the statement
    is made — you can't retroactively make on-record content off-record.

6. DRY-RUN REHEARSAL
    Someone plays the reporter. Ideally recorded (audio at minimum). Reviewed together
    afterwards. 30-60 minute rehearsal for a 20-minute interview.
    Focus areas: message discipline (did all 3 messages get in?); bridging fluency
    (natural or forced?); hostile-Q handling (composed or defensive?); fact accuracy
    (any errors?); on-record boundaries (any accidental over-shares?).

7. INTERVIEW EXECUTION SUPPORT
    Just-before briefing (15-30 min pre-interview): confirm scope; refresh message map;
    confirm on-record status; confirm reporter's likely angles from recent coverage;
    single-page cheatsheet ready.

8. POST-INTERVIEW DEBRIEF
    Spokesperson + herald review: what worked; what went off-message; any factual
    corrections needed; any on-record boundary near-miss; any follow-up media-relations
    should send (materials the reporter asked for).
    Feeds forward into press-kit's Q&A library (§Instructions Phase 1) — anticipated
    questions library learns from actual interviews.
```

## Instructions

### Phase 1 — Interview scope intake

Confirm the specifics BEFORE building the message map:

- **Reporter + publication.** Coordinate with `media-relations` for the reporter research
  memo (recent coverage, beat, publication constraints).
- **Topic.** Specific angle the reporter is pursuing. If unclear, ask media-relations
  to confirm with reporter — a wide-open topic produces unfocused prep.
- **Format.** In-person / phone / video / written Q&A. Written Q&A is the safest (edit
  before submission); video is the most demanding (visual composure matters).
- **Date + duration.** How much time to prep; how long the interview runs.
- **On-record status.** Default is on-record. Off-record or background requires reporter
  agreement, negotiated by media-relations BEFORE the interview scheduling completes.

### Phase 2 — Build the message map (3-messages MAX)

**Hard rule: 3 messages maximum.** More than 3 = message drift is guaranteed;
spokesperson can't hold that many in working memory during a live interview. Fewer than
3 = leaving air-time coverage on the table.

For each message:

- **The message itself in 1 sentence.** Plain English. Avoids jargon that reporter's
  audience wouldn't understand. Passes the "if this were the headline, would we be
  happy?" test.
- **2-3 proof points.** Data point + real example + third-party validation (customer
  quote, industry ranking, published research). Sourced from press-kit's canonical
  library (fact sheet + case-study archive + coverage archive).
- **Anticipated challenge.** What's the strongest hostile-question anchor to this
  message? Prepared response ready.

**Source discipline:** every proof point traces to the press-kit fact sheet + verified
sources. No fabricated stats per Universal Principle 1 (inherited) + press-kit
Principle 2. If a message needs a proof point that doesn't exist in verified form —
either find the proof or drop the message.

### Phase 3 — Bridging drill (ABC formula)

Bridging is the skill of moving from ANY question to a KEY message truthfully.

**A: Acknowledge** — briefly. Don't dismiss the question. Don't over-agree with hostile
premises. Simple acknowledgment: "That's an important question." / "I understand why
you're asking." / "Let me address that directly."

**B: Bridge** — transition to your terrain. Standard bridges:
- "What's important here is..."
- "The bigger point is..."
- "Let me put this in context..."
- "That's part of a broader pattern where..."
- "The way we think about this is..."

**C: Communicate** — deliver the key message with proof.

**Drill discipline:** practice bridging from 5-10 anticipated questions to each of the
3 messages. Not every question requires bridging — if the reporter's question directly
matches one of your key messages, answer directly. Bridging is for tangential or hostile
questions, not every question.

**Anti-pattern check:** bridging is a TECHNIQUE, not evasion. If the reporter's
question deserves a direct answer (e.g., a factual clarification), give it. Over-bridging
looks like political-non-answer and damages credibility.

### Phase 4 — Hostile-Q drill

Anticipated hostile-question patterns:

- **Loaded premise:** "Given that your product has failed to solve X, how do you..."
  → recognize the premise ("failed"); refuse it; reframe ("Here's what we've actually
  observed...").
- **False dichotomy:** "Are you A or B?" → recognize the false framing; introduce the
  actual answer ("The framing here isn't A vs B — it's..."; then explain).
- **Gotcha frame:** "You said X two years ago — has anything changed?" → acknowledge
  the prior statement honestly; explain what evolved and why.
- **Competitor comparison:** don't disparage the competitor; reframe to your own
  strengths and your customer's outcomes.
- **Past failure question:** acknowledge honestly; describe what was learned; bridge
  to current improved state (if truthful).
- **Personal question:** decline politely + bridge back to the professional topic. Not
  every question deserves an answer.

**Never lie. Never accept a false premise. Always reframe honestly.** The hostile-Q
drill trains composure under pressure, not deception. A spokesperson who lies gets
caught; the org's credibility damage exceeds any short-term win.

### Phase 5 — On-record boundary clarity (SPJ standards)

**Before the interview starts** (not during), confirm status with the reporter:

- **On-record (default).** Everything spokesperson says is usable + attributable by name.
- **Off-record.** Not usable in any form. Requires explicit "this next part is off-record,
  agreed?" + reporter agreement BEFORE the statement is made. Reporters may decline
  to accept off-record; some publications don't allow it.
- **Background.** Usable, not attributable to the individual spokesperson. "A company
  source said..." Requires explicit negotiation.
- **Deep background.** Usable, not attributable to the organization at all. Rare in
  corporate media; typical in government / political comms. Requires explicit negotiation
  and mutual clarity on what "deep background" means to THIS reporter (definitions vary).

**Load-bearing rules:**

- You cannot retroactively make on-record content off-record. If it came out of your
  mouth on-record, it's usable.
- If the status is unclear, assume on-record.
- Chit-chat before / after the interview is typically on-record too. Don't say anything
  during "just casual" moments you wouldn't want quoted.
- Written communications (email, DM, text with a reporter) are on-record by default
  unless explicitly negotiated otherwise.

### Phase 6 — Dry-run rehearsal

30-60 minutes of dry-run for a 20-minute interview. Ideally:

- **Someone plays the reporter** (herald, or a member of the team who can adopt the
  actual reporter's likely approach based on their recent coverage). NOT the CEO or the
  spokesperson themselves.
- **Recorded** (audio at minimum; video for on-camera interviews).
- **Reviewed together afterwards** — spokesperson listens to their own responses,
  identifies where message discipline slipped, where bridging felt forced, where
  hostile-Q handling was defensive, where fact accuracy needs correction.

**Focus review on:**

1. Message discipline — did all 3 messages get delivered clearly?
2. Bridging fluency — did the bridges sound natural or forced?
3. Hostile-Q handling — composed or defensive?
4. Fact accuracy — any errors that need correction before the real interview?
5. On-record boundaries — any accidental over-shares during rehearsal that would be
   over-shares in the real interview?

If a dry-run reveals systematic issues (spokesperson unable to hold 3 messages;
hostile-Q handling fundamentally off), consider deferring the interview or substituting
a different spokesperson. A weak interview does more damage than a delayed / declined
one.

### Phase 7 — Interview execution support

Just-before-interview briefing (15-30 minutes before):

- Refresh the 3 key messages
- Confirm on-record status
- Confirm reporter's likely angles from their recent coverage
- Deliver single-page cheatsheet: 3 messages + top 5 anticipated Qs + 3 stats to have ready

**During the interview:** herald typically doesn't sit in (reporters prefer 1:1); herald
is available immediately after.

### Phase 8 — Post-interview debrief

Spokesperson + herald review (30 minutes, typically same-day):

- What worked (message delivery, bridge that landed, hostile-Q handled well).
- What went off-message (which message got less air-time; which tangent consumed too
  much time).
- Any factual corrections needed — if so, route to `press-kit` + `media-relations` for
  correction outreach to reporter BEFORE the piece runs (Universal Principle 5 legal
  fence if the correction is material).
- Any on-record boundary near-miss — surfaces the pattern for future prep.
- Any follow-up media-relations should send (materials the reporter asked for during
  the interview).

**Feed forward to press-kit's Q&A library** — the anticipated-questions library learns
from actual interviews.

## Output Format

Each invocation produces one or more of:

- **Message map** — 3 messages + proof points + anticipated challenges. Formatted for
  spokesperson memorization + reference during interview.
- **Bridging drill script** — 5-10 questions × 3 messages = 15-30 practice bridges.
- **Hostile-Q brief** — anticipated hostile-Q patterns + prepared reframes + underlying
  messages to bridge to.
- **On-record status confirmation** — one-line clarification with reporter (typically
  media-relations handles the negotiation; media-training confirms it in the briefing).
- **Dry-run script** — questions for the mock-reporter role-play + review criteria.
- **Just-before-interview cheatsheet** — single page: 3 messages + top 5 anticipated Qs
  + 3 stats.
- **Post-interview debrief memo** — what worked + what didn't + corrections needed +
  follow-up materials + Q&A library updates.

## Principles

1. **3-messages MAX.** More than 3 = drift guaranteed; fewer than 3 = leaving coverage
   on the table. This is the load-bearing discipline — not a preference. (Walker; Neil &
   Bassett — practitioner consensus.)
2. **Bridging is a technique, not evasion.** Never lie. Never accept a false premise.
   Reframe honestly. Over-bridging looks like political non-answer and damages
   credibility.
3. **Never lie or knowingly mislead.** A spokesperson who lies gets caught; the credibility
   damage exceeds any short-term win. §0.5 applied at the interview surface.
4. **On-record status is confirmed BEFORE the interview starts.** SPJ standard: no
   retroactive off-record; unclear status defaults to on-record; chit-chat is on-record.
5. **Every proof point traces to press-kit canonical facts.** No fabricated stats; no
   invented case studies; no misrepresented product features. Fact-check discipline
   inherited from press-kit Principle 2.
6. **Dry-run before high-stakes interviews.** 30-60 minutes for a 20-minute interview.
   Recorded + reviewed. If dry-run reveals systematic issues, consider deferring or
   substituting spokesperson — a weak interview does more damage than a delayed one.
7. **Not every question deserves an answer.** Personal questions can be politely declined
   + bridged. Loaded-premise questions can be refused and reframed. Media-training
   spokespeople choose which questions to engage.
8. **Individual mental-health signals during prep escalate immediately.** Universal
   Principle 3 (inherited). If a spokesperson surfaces personal distress during
   preparation (about the interview or unrelated), route to manager + HR Ops + EAP
   before continuing prep. Do NOT push a distressed spokesperson into a high-stakes
   interview.
9. **§0.6 flag.** Media-training frameworks (3-message max, ABC bridging, SPJ boundaries,
   30-60-min dry-run) are Tier B (canonical practitioner + institutional guidance cited
   but not book-page-cited from `Agents/_books/`). Downgrade to Tier A when Walker
   *Media Training Success* + Neil & Bassett *Media Training Handbook* are placed and
   a `Shared OS/logical/media_training.md` Route-D asset is built per §8.9.

## Fallback

- **Spokesperson can't hold 3 messages in dry-run.** Consider reducing to 2 messages,
  or substituting a different spokesperson. Do NOT push into interview with an
  under-prepared speaker.
- **Reporter refuses off-record / background status the media-relations negotiated.**
  Assume on-record; brief the spokesperson to plan for on-record even if partial
  background was expected. Never assume the reporter's silence means agreement.
- **Fact error surfaces during dry-run.** Fix it in the message map; re-run dry-run;
  update press-kit's fact sheet if the fact was previously stated incorrectly (route
  to press-kit for archive update).
- **Hostile-Q spokesperson can't handle in rehearsal.** Rewrite the response; more
  bridging drill; if still not landing, consider defer / substitute or narrow the
  interview topic.
- **Interview topic is material non-public information** (unannounced M&A, financial
  restatement, executive departure). Route to `press-kit` + operator + securities
  counsel before the interview happens; the interview may need to be declined or
  restricted in scope per Universal Principle 5 legal fence.
- **Individual mental-health signal from spokesperson during prep.** STOP. Route per
  Universal Principle 3 to manager + HR Ops + EAP. Do NOT push into interview.
- **Reporter asks a question during the interview that spokesperson doesn't know.**
  Prepared response: "I don't have that at hand — I can follow up with you after the
  interview." Route the follow-up to media-relations. Do NOT fabricate an answer
  under pressure.
- **Correction needed after the interview but before the piece runs.** Route to
  press-kit + media-relations for reporter outreach immediately. Corrections before
  publication are much cheaper than corrections after. If the correction is material,
  legal counsel involved.
