# Change Comms

## Introduction

This skill covers **change-management communications** — the distinct discipline required
when the internal event is a REORG, LAYOFF, MERGER, MAJOR RESTRUCTURE, or other MAJOR
TRANSITION. Distinct from routine internal-cadence (weekly notes, monthly all-hands,
decision broadcasts) because change events combine legal-adjacent exposure + emotional
weight + long timelines + high risk of misinterpretation.

Built from scratch per §2 roster expansion. Sources: Kotter's 8-step change model
(academic anchor), Bridges' transition model (distinguishes external change from
internal psychological transition), Prosci ADKAR (individual-change methodology), and
McCord's *Powerful* (direct-but-honest layoff voice, already cited across P&C).

**Load-bearing scope boundary:** change-comms handles the COMMS discipline for
change events. It does NOT handle:
- Legal-formalization of layoffs / restructures → operator + employment counsel per
  Universal Principle 5 legal fence. WARN Act notices, protected-class impact analysis,
  severance-agreement language, and international jurisdiction requirements are legal
  work, not comms work.
- Structural design of the reorg / workforce plan → hire's `workforce-planning` +
  merit's `succession-planning`.
- Individual crisis signals during change conversations → HARD BOUNDARY escalation per
  Universal Principle 3.

## Purpose

Prevents five failure modes that show up when major-change comms operate without
distinct discipline:

1. **Change-as-directive** — leadership announces the change without pre-comms narrative
   or post-comms reinforcement; team receives it as arbitrary. Kotter's 8-step model
   addresses this: step 1 is "create a sense of urgency" (pre-comms), step 8 is
   "anchor changes in corporate culture" (post-comms).
2. **Confused audience segmentation** — same layoff comms message goes to affected
   employees + retained employees + external partners + customers. Each audience needs
   distinct framing with distinct actionable content. Undifferentiated comms damages
   trust in multiple directions simultaneously.
3. **Bridges' Neutral Zone unmanaged** — announcement happens (Ending); new state
   arrives eventually (New Beginning); but the middle period (Neutral Zone — high
   uncertainty, low productivity, high anxiety) gets ignored. Bridges argues most
   change-management failures happen in the Neutral Zone, not at Ending or New Beginning.
4. **Legal-formalization confused with comms** — comms person drafts language that
   creates legal exposure (WARN Act violations, protected-class-adjacent phrasing,
   severance-negotiation-adjacent statements). Legal fence per Universal Principle 5 —
   employment counsel involved BEFORE any change comms ships.
5. **Individual mental-health signals during change conversations** — layoffs and major
   reorgs surface distress. HARD BOUNDARY escalation per Universal Principle 3 (inherited).

signal uses this skill whenever an internal event qualifies as major-change (reorg /
layoff / merger / major restructure) — distinct from the routine cadence that
`internal-cadence` (sibling) owns.

## Structure / Protocol

The change-comms workflow combines three frameworks:

```
KOTTER 8-STEP CHANGE MODEL (organizational-change process)
  1. Create a sense of urgency          (pre-comms narrative)
  2. Build a guiding coalition          (leadership alignment)
  3. Form a strategic vision            (what the new state looks like)
  4. Enlist a volunteer army            (early adopters + advocates)
  5. Enable action by removing barriers (unblock the transition)
  6. Generate short-term wins           (visible progress)
  7. Sustain acceleration               (build on wins)
  8. Institute change                   (anchor in culture)

BRIDGES TRANSITION MODEL (individual-psychological transition)
  Phase 1: ENDING          — letting go of the old (grief; loss; identity change)
  Phase 2: NEUTRAL ZONE    — the messy middle (uncertainty; ambiguity; low productivity)
  Phase 3: NEW BEGINNING   — embracing the new (energy; commitment; identity formation)

PROSCI ADKAR (individual-change milestones — sequential)
  A: AWARENESS      — of the need for change
  D: DESIRE         — to support the change (voluntary participation)
  K: KNOWLEDGE      — of how to change (skills + information)
  A: ABILITY        — to demonstrate new skills + behaviors
  R: REINFORCEMENT  — to sustain the change

CHANGE-COMMS SEQUENCE (this skill's operational workflow):
  1. Confirm scope + change type (reorg / layoff / merger / transition)
  2. Confirm legal-fence status  — employment counsel involved BEFORE comms ships
  3. Segment audiences           — affected / retained / customers / external
  4. Draft pre-change narrative  — Kotter step 1 + Sinek Start-With-Why + Bridges Ending
  5. Draft announcement          — audience-specific; honest WHY; explicit
                                   WHAT-CHANGES per audience
  6. Design Neutral Zone comms   — high-cadence updates during the messy middle
                                   (Bridges' most-often-neglected phase)
  7. Design reinforcement comms  — Kotter step 8 + ADKAR reinforcement; post-change
                                   institutional-anchoring
  8. Archive + close-the-loop    — per internal-cadence archive discipline
```

## Instructions

### Phase 1 — Confirm scope + change type

Confirm the change qualifies as major-change requiring this skill's discipline (vs
routine decision broadcast which routes to `internal-cadence`):

- **Reorg** — change in reporting lines, team structure, function boundaries, or
  leadership responsibilities affecting >2-3 people.
- **Layoff / RIF** — involuntary departures beyond individual performance-management.
- **Merger / acquisition** — M&A affecting company structure, ownership, or
  employment.
- **Major transition** — office relocation, business-model shift, product-line
  discontinuation, or any change that substantially changes work for a meaningful
  cohort.

If the change doesn't qualify, route to `internal-cadence`'s decision-broadcast structure
(smaller, faster, less discipline overhead).

### Phase 2 — Confirm legal-fence status BEFORE any drafting

**Universal Principle 5 legal fence LOAD-BEARING for change-comms.** Employment counsel
must be involved BEFORE any change-comms ships. Specifically:

- **Layoffs / RIF** — WARN Act (US federal + state-specific) notification requirements;
  protected-class impact analysis; severance-agreement language; international
  jurisdiction requirements (varying notice periods, works-council consultation
  requirements in EU, etc.).
- **Reorg with role eliminations** — same as layoff subset.
- **Merger / acquisition** — securities-law disclosure timing (SEC Reg FD for public
  companies), employment-contract implications for affected employees, works-council
  requirements.
- **Major transition** — location-specific employment-law implications (e.g., office
  relocation triggering constructive-dismissal claims).

If employment counsel is NOT involved, HOLD the comms drafting. Route to operator to
engage counsel before proceeding.

### Phase 3 — Segment audiences

Change comms almost never uses a single message. Standard audience segments:

- **Affected employees** (layoff targets, reorged-out roles) — first + separate + most
  compassionate + most operational (severance + timing + next-steps clarity).
- **Directly affected employees** (retained but role-changed by the reorg) — WHAT this
  changes for you specifically + support during Neutral Zone.
- **Retained employees** (not directly affected but part of same team / function) —
  what changes for the team + reassurance about future stability + honest information
  about why colleagues are leaving.
- **Adjacent teams** (downstream / upstream teams affected by the reorg) — how
  handoffs change + who to contact for what.
- **Customers / partners** — external-facing narrative (routes to herald's `media-relations`
  + `press-kit` for external drafting; signal coordinates for internal-external
  consistency).
- **Board / investors** — material-info disclosure (routes to beacon's `investor-cadence`
  + operator + securities counsel).

Each segment gets DISTINCT framing. Same message to all segments = misinterpretation in
multiple directions.

### Phase 4 — Draft pre-change narrative (Kotter step 1 + Bridges Ending)

**Weeks-to-days BEFORE announcement**, if the change is planned + non-secret:

- **Sense of urgency** (Kotter step 1) — why the org needs to change now. Business
  driver, market context, strategic pivot. Honest. Not "we're growing so fast we need
  to restructure" (which is often false); instead the real reason.
- **Guiding coalition** (Kotter step 2) — leadership alignment on the change. Change
  comms fail when leadership publicly aligns but privately dissents; alignment must be
  genuine before comms ships.
- **Strategic vision** (Kotter step 3) — what the new state looks like. Concrete enough
  that people can picture their role in it.

For layoffs specifically, the pre-change narrative is often limited (surprise
announcement is required for legal + security reasons — WARN Act timing rules,
insider-trading concerns for public companies). Legal-fence phase 2 determines what
pre-comms is permitted.

### Phase 5 — Draft announcement (audience-specific + honest WHY + WHAT-CHANGES)

Per Phase 3 audience segments, draft distinct announcements:

**Structure per announcement (inherits from `internal-cadence` decision-broadcast
structure but expanded):**

- **WHAT changed** — concrete + specific.
- **WHY it changed** — honest + non-euphemistic. Corporate euphemism erodes trust
  more than the underlying news does (McCord discipline; already anchored in Scott
  identity + hire principles).
- **WHAT this changes for YOU** — per-audience specific.
- **TIMING** — dates, sequences, milestones.
- **RESOURCES** — where to get more information, who to contact, EAP availability
  for affected employees, transition support.
- **NEXT COMMUNICATION** — when the next update will come + through what channel.

For layoff communications specifically, McCord's discipline: **direct, plain English,
adult presumption, no euphemism, treat affected people with respect + as adults.** The
McCord voice is inherited from Scott identity via department-leader inheritance +
already-anchored across P&C.

### Phase 6 — Design Neutral Zone comms (Bridges' most-neglected phase)

**The Neutral Zone is where most change-management fails.** Between announcement
(Ending) and stable new state (New Beginning), teams experience:

- High uncertainty about role, priorities, reporting relationships
- Low productivity (measurable; Bridges cites 30-50% temporary decline)
- High anxiety and turnover risk
- Rumor generation to fill information gaps

**High-cadence updates DURING the Neutral Zone** — much more frequent than routine
internal-cadence:

- Weekly (minimum) written updates from change leadership on progress + open questions
  + what's still uncertain
- Regular office-hours or Q&A forums where team can ask questions
- Explicit acknowledgment of the difficulty ("this is a hard time; we know") —
  performative acknowledgment fails; genuine acknowledgment builds trust
- Named point-of-contact for questions per affected group

Skipping Neutral Zone comms = the change fails in practice even if the org-chart-level
change succeeds.

### Phase 7 — Design reinforcement comms (Kotter step 8 + ADKAR Reinforcement)

**Weeks-to-months AFTER the New Beginning starts**, reinforce the change through:

- **Short-term wins broadcast** (Kotter step 6) — visible progress in the new state.
- **Institutional anchoring** (Kotter step 8) — new processes, new team names, new
  meeting cadences, new success metrics. Comms references the new state as normal;
  stops referring to the old state as reference point.
- **Reinforcement per ADKAR** — celebrate examples of desired new behavior; correct
  regression to old behavior; make the new state the default.

Without reinforcement, teams drift back to old behaviors within 6-12 months even after
apparently-successful change.

### Phase 8 — Archive + close-the-loop (per internal-cadence discipline)

- All change-comms artifacts to searchable archive (per `internal-cadence` Phase 5
  archive discipline).
- Close-the-loop discipline continues during + after change: visible actions from
  previous cycle's feedback communicated before next update (per `internal-cadence`
  Phase 6 minimum-viable-action rule).
- Post-change retrospective: what worked / what didn't / lessons for next change event.

## Output Format

Each invocation produces one or more of:

- **Change-scope confirmation memo** — is this major-change requiring change-comms
  discipline, or routine decision routing to `internal-cadence`?
- **Legal-fence status check** — employment counsel involvement confirmed for the change
  type before drafting proceeds.
- **Audience segmentation plan** — per Phase 3.
- **Pre-change narrative brief** — Kotter step 1-3 (urgency + coalition + vision) where
  pre-comms is permitted.
- **Announcement drafts** (audience-specific) — WHAT / WHY / WHAT-CHANGES-FOR-YOU /
  TIMING / RESOURCES / NEXT COMMUNICATION.
- **Neutral Zone comms plan** — cadence + channels + named-point-of-contact per group +
  Q&A forum design.
- **Reinforcement plan** — Kotter step 8 + ADKAR reinforcement.
- **Post-change retrospective** — what worked + what didn't + lessons for next change.

## Principles

1. **Legal fence BEFORE comms drafting.** Universal Principle 5 (inherited). Employment
   counsel involved before any change-comms ships. WARN Act, protected-class impact,
   severance language, international requirements are LEGAL work; do not draft comms
   language that creates legal exposure.
2. **Audience segmentation is mandatory.** Same message to all audiences = misinterpretation
   in multiple directions. Affected / retained / adjacent / external / board — each
   distinct.
3. **Honest WHY, no corporate euphemism.** Inherited from `internal-cadence` Principle
   3; sharpened here — "headwinds," "efficiency measures," "strategic reallocation,"
   "personnel adjustments" during a layoff erode trust more than the underlying news
   does. McCord discipline (inherited via Scott identity + P&C).
4. **Neutral Zone comms is the highest-leverage phase.** Most change failures happen
   between announcement and new stable state (Bridges). High-cadence updates during
   the Neutral Zone are non-optional — skipping them = the change fails even if the
   structural change succeeds.
5. **Reinforcement lasts weeks-to-months, not days.** Kotter step 8 + ADKAR Reinforcement
   sustain the change. Without reinforcement, teams drift back to old behaviors within
   6-12 months.
6. **Individual mental-health signals during change conversations escalate immediately.**
   Universal Principle 3 (inherited). Change events surface distress; HARD BOUNDARY to
   manager + HR Ops + EAP.
7. **Aggregate-only at publication surface.** Universal Principle 7 (inherited). Change
   comms may name individual leadership (attribution for decisions) but never publish
   individual perf data, individual severance details, or individual demographic data.
8. **Change-comms does NOT substitute for structural or legal work.** Structural design
   → hire's workforce-planning + merit's succession-planning. Legal formalization →
   operator + employment counsel. This skill covers COMMS discipline only.
9. **§0.6 flag.** Kotter 8-step + Bridges 3-phase + Prosci ADKAR are Tier B (canonical
   published frameworks cited but not book-page-cited from `Agents/_books/`). Downgrade
   to Tier A when Kotter 2012 + Bridges 2009 + Prosci institutional materials are
   placed and a `Shared OS/logical/change_comms.md` Route-D asset is built per §8.9.

## Fallback

- **Legal counsel not involved.** HOLD drafting. Route to operator to engage counsel.
  Comms language that creates legal exposure is worse than delayed comms.
- **Change scope unclear** (is this major-change or routine?). Ask requester + err on
  the side of change-comms discipline for anything affecting >2-3 people substantially.
  Over-formalizing change comms is cheap; under-formalizing burns trust.
- **Pre-change narrative not permitted** (surprise required for legal / security /
  insider-trading reasons). Accept the constraint; move directly to Phase 5 (announcement)
  + Phase 6 (Neutral Zone comms). Note the lack of pre-comms in the change retrospective.
- **Audience segmentation collapses** (operator wants "one message for everyone" for
  speed). Push back per Principle 2 — undifferentiated change comms damages trust in
  multiple directions. If operator insists, ship with explicit note that segmentation
  was skipped and this is expected to reduce message effectiveness.
- **Neutral Zone comms plan requires more resource than available.** Escalate — recommend
  reducing the change scope OR extending the timeline OR investing in additional
  change-comms resource. Skipping Neutral Zone comms = the change fails.
- **Euphemism creeping in from executive drafts.** Push back per Principle 3 — provide
  direct plain-English alternatives + explain the trust cost of euphemism.
- **Individual mental-health signal during change conversation.** STOP. Route per
  Universal Principle 3 to manager + HR Ops + EAP.
- **Change comms requested for external audiences** (customers / partners / press). Route
  to herald's `press-kit` + `media-relations` for external drafting; signal coordinates
  for internal-external consistency but does NOT draft external-facing change comms.
- **Material NPI in change announcement** (M&A, financial restatement, executive
  departure). Route to beacon + operator + securities counsel BEFORE internal broadcast.
  Internal announcement timing coordinates with external disclosure timing per SEC Reg
  FD for public companies.
