# Go-To-Market Adaptation

## Introduction

This skill packages the GTM adaptation discipline for compass — AFTER
`market-selection-framework` (Phase 1) + `entry-mode-decision` (Phase 2)
produce the chosen market + mode. Ghemawat's AAA framework (Adaptation /
Aggregation / Arbitrage) applied to GTM decisions + Douglas & Craig global-
marketing methodology + Kotler & Keller 4Ps/STP foundation + Meyer's 8-scale
cultural framework for business-communication adaptation.

**Scope distinction:** this is GTM ADAPTATION for an already-chosen market +
mode — product / pricing / positioning / channel adaptation per market.
Distinct from `market-selection-framework` (Phase 1 — chose country),
`entry-mode-decision` (Phase 2 — chose mode), and `expansion-portfolio-mgmt`
(Phase 4+ — multi-market rebalancing).

Reclassified from a marketplace scope-mismatch per §4.6.

Custom Route D per §8.2 — cited rubric grounded in Ghemawat's canonical
AAA-framework corpus.

## Purpose

Prevents six failure modes that show up when GTM adaptation is unstructured:

1. **Home-market GTM copy-paste.** Applying home-market product / pricing /
   messaging / channel mix without adaptation to local market = predictable
   underperformance. Ghemawat + Douglas & Craig both document the pattern.
2. **AAA-blind strategy.** Defaulting to Adaptation (customize per market)
   when Aggregation (regional standardization) or Arbitrage (exploit
   cross-country differences in costs / regulations) would create more
   value. Ghemawat's insight: firms consistently over-adapt when aggregation
   or arbitrage could work.
3. **Pricing set without purchasing-power + competitive benchmark.**
   Global-uniform pricing in low-purchasing-power markets prices out demand;
   in high-purchasing-power markets leaves value on the table.
4. **Culturally-blind messaging.** Direct-translation marketing messages
   without cultural adaptation — misses Meyer's 8-scale differences (direct
   vs. indirect communication, principles-first vs. applications-first,
   task-based vs. relationship-based trust, etc.).
5. **Channel-strategy mismatch with entry-mode.** GTM channel plan
   inconsistent with entry-mode decision from Phase 2 (e.g., direct-sales
   GTM after choosing distributor entry mode).
6. **Individual crisis DURING GTM adaptation crunch.** Team members under
   launch-timing pressure + personal distress can coincide. HARD BOUNDARY
   per Universal Principle 3.

compass uses this skill as Phase 3 of any expansion decision. Coordinates
downstream with lingua (marketing / product / cultural adaptation), frontier
(cross-border pricing operations if applicable), signal (internal launch
announcement), and herald / beacon (external launch announcement if material).

## Structure / Protocol

The GTM-adaptation workflow combines AAA + Kotler 4Ps + Meyer cultural framework:

```
GHEMAWAT AAA FRAMEWORK (Ghemawat 2007, Chapter 6)

  ADAPTATION      — customize per market (product / pricing / positioning /
                    channel adjusted to local preferences + constraints)
                    Value driver: relevance to local demand
                    Cost: complexity + reduced scale economies

  AGGREGATION     — standardize across markets (regional or global common
                    platform / product / pricing / positioning where
                    tolerable)
                    Value driver: scale economies + brand consistency
                    Cost: reduced local relevance

  ARBITRAGE       — exploit cross-country differences (source production
                    from low-cost country; sell in high-price country;
                    tax / regulatory arbitrage; talent arbitrage)
                    Value driver: cost differential capture
                    Cost: supply-chain complexity + regulatory-arbitrage
                          risk

  Ghemawat's insight: most firms over-focus on Adaptation. Aggregation +
  Arbitrage often create more value when properly assessed.

  AAA choice is NEVER pure — always a mix; the question is WHICH DOMINATES
  for this specific market + business.


KOTLER 4Ps + STP (Kotler & Keller)

  PRODUCT           — feature set, packaging, quality tier, service bundle
  PRICE             — level, structure (subscription / one-time / freemium),
                     discount structure
  PLACE (Channel)   — distribution / sales channels, retail vs. direct,
                     partner-driven vs. own-channel
  PROMOTION         — messaging, media mix, PR, digital, events

  STP:
  Segmentation     — how to segment the new market's customer base
  Targeting        — which segments to prioritize (usually a subset)
  Positioning      — how the product positions in the target segment's mind


MEYER 8-SCALE CULTURAL FRAMEWORK (Meyer 2014)

  For adapting BUSINESS COMMUNICATION + POSITIONING per culture:

  Communicating       low-context <—> high-context
  Evaluating          direct negative feedback <—> indirect
  Persuading          principles-first <—> applications-first
  Leading             egalitarian <—> hierarchical
  Deciding            consensual <—> top-down
  Trusting            task-based <—> relationship-based
  Disagreeing         confrontational <—> avoids confrontation
  Scheduling          linear-time <—> flexible-time

  Positioning + messaging tuned to target market's 8-scale profile.


GO-TO-MARKET ADAPTATION OPERATIONAL SEQUENCE (this skill's phase-by-phase):

  Phase 1: AAA-STRATEGY DECISION                (per market — which dominates?)
  Phase 2: PRODUCT ADAPTATION SCOPING            (features / packaging / positioning)
  Phase 3: PRICING ADAPTATION                     (level / structure with PP + competitive benchmark)
  Phase 4: CHANNEL / DISTRIBUTION ADAPTATION      (per market + consistent with entry-mode)
  Phase 5: MARKETING-MESSAGE ADAPTATION SCOPING   (Meyer 8-scale → messaging brief)
  Phase 6: GTM LAUNCH PLAN + CROSS-AGENT HANDOFFS
```

## Instructions

### Phase 1 — AAA-strategy decision per market

For this market + business combination, assess which AAA dominates:

- **Adaptation candidates** — product where local preferences differ materially
  (food, fashion, culturally-specific services), highly-regulated products
  (healthcare, financial services), locally-specific use cases
- **Aggregation candidates** — global brands with strong global recognition,
  digital products with low-marginal-cost delivery, B2B enterprise with
  standardized decision-makers globally
- **Arbitrage candidates** — cost-arbitrage (production in low-cost country
  for sale in high-price country), tax-arbitrage (with counsel — Universal
  Principle 5), talent-arbitrage (engineering in low-cost country for global
  product), regulatory-arbitrage (with counsel — high-risk)

Score each AAA on a 1-5 scale for the specific market + business. Highest-
scoring AAA dominates; usually a MIX with one dominant.

Document AAA choice with explicit rationale — silent over-adaptation is the
default failure mode Ghemawat warns against.

### Phase 2 — Product adaptation scoping

Given AAA choice, scope product-side adaptation:

- **Feature set** — which features stay standard (aggregation) vs. adapt
  (adaptation)? Cite market-research signals (customer interviews, competitor
  analysis, local usage patterns).
- **Packaging** — physical (retail markets, size / language / regulation) or
  digital (app-store presentation, onboarding flow, feature-flag
  differentiation).
- **Positioning** — product positioning in local competitive context. Cite
  local competitor set + local customer decision-criteria.
- **Service bundle** — support hours, language coverage, SLA tier per market
  (coordinate with future Client Success dept).

Handoff to lingua `product-localization` for technical i18n execution.

### Phase 3 — Pricing adaptation

- **Purchasing-power adjustment.** Cite PPP data (IMF WEO, World Bank PPP
  conversion factor). Global-uniform pricing in low-PP markets = demand
  destruction; in high-PP markets = leaving value on table.
- **Competitive benchmark.** Cite local competitor pricing at comparable
  quality/feature tier.
- **Structure.** Subscription / one-time / freemium / usage-based — may vary
  per market based on customer purchasing habits + local payment
  infrastructure (coordinate with frontier `cross-border-payments`).
- **Discount / promo structure.** Local competitive-response + retail-partner
  discount norms if applicable.
- **NEVER fabricate PP or competitor pricing numbers.** Universal Principle 1.

### Phase 4 — Channel / distribution adaptation

- **Consistency with entry-mode from Phase 2 skill.** Distributor entry mode
  → channel plan works THROUGH distributor. Direct-sales / greenfield entry
  mode → own-channel plan. JV entry mode → JV-channel-agreement.
- **Local channel norms.** Some markets are retail-heavy (physical retail
  dominance), some are e-commerce-native (mobile-first), some are relationship-
  channel-driven (business partners / consultants as primary channel).
- **Media mix** — local media consumption patterns; digital-vs-traditional split
  varies materially per market.
- **Retail-partner selection** if applicable — coordinate with entry-mode
  distributor / partner shortlist.

### Phase 5 — Marketing-message adaptation scoping (Meyer 8-scale)

For this market's cultural profile, assess where it sits on Meyer's 8 scales.
Adapt messaging + positioning per scale:

- **Communicating** — low-context markets tolerate direct messaging;
  high-context markets need indirect, layered messaging with implicit meaning
- **Persuading** — principles-first markets (e.g., much of Europe) want the
  "why" before the "what"; applications-first markets (US-style B2B) want the
  "what" upfront
- **Trusting** — task-based markets (US) can be sold to on features + pricing;
  relationship-based markets need trust-building + relationship-investment
  before feature-selling
- **Deciding** — top-down markets: sell to the decision-maker directly;
  consensual markets: sell to the group

Messaging brief output → handoff to lingua `marketing-localization` for
execution.

### Phase 6 — GTM launch plan + cross-agent handoffs

**GTM launch plan** (4-8 pages) combining Phases 1-5:

- AAA strategy per market with rationale
- Product adaptation scope
- Pricing per market with PP + competitive benchmark
- Channel plan consistent with entry mode
- Messaging brief per Meyer cultural profile
- Launch timeline + milestone gates
- Cross-agent handoffs

**Handoffs**:
- lingua `product-localization` — technical i18n execution
- lingua `marketing-localization` — messaging translation + cultural adaptation
- lingua `cultural-adaptation` — deeper cultural-adaptation work (Hofstede +
  Meyer)
- lingua `legal-localization` — legal-document adaptation if product-legal
  (T&Cs, privacy policy) is user-facing
- frontier `cross-border-payments` + `international-banking` — payment
  infrastructure for pricing structure
- signal `internal-cadence` + `change-comms` — internal launch announcement
- herald `press-kit` + `media-relations` — external launch announcement if
  press-worthy
- beacon `investor-cadence` — investor coordination if launch is material
  (Reg FD fence)

## Output Format

Each invocation produces one or more of:

- **AAA-strategy decision memo** — per market, which AAA dominates + rationale
- **Product-adaptation scope memo** — feature set / packaging / positioning /
  service bundle
- **Pricing memo** — level / structure with cited PP + competitive benchmark
- **Channel plan** — consistent with entry-mode; local channel norms addressed
- **Messaging brief** — Meyer 8-scale profile → per-scale messaging adaptation
- **GTM launch plan** — 4-8 pages combining Phases 1-5 + timeline + milestones
- **Handoff briefs** to lingua + frontier + signal + herald + beacon (as
  applicable)

## Principles

1. **Never home-market copy-paste.** AAA analysis mandatory Phase 1.
2. **AAA-strategy documented explicitly.** Silent over-adaptation = §Principles
   violation.
3. **Pricing cites PP + competitive benchmark.** Fabricated pricing numbers =
   Universal Principle 1 violation.
4. **Meyer 8-scale cultural profile applied to messaging.** Skipping =
   culturally-blind messaging failure mode.
5. **Channel plan consistent with entry-mode from Phase 2 skill.**
   Inconsistency = internal contradiction.
6. **No fabrication** — cited sources for PP (IMF WEO / World Bank), competitor
   pricing (public sources), market-research signals (interview / study
   references), cultural-profile mapping (Meyer / Hofstede).
7. **Aggregate-only at publication surface** — Universal Principle 2.
8. **Individual crisis HARD BOUNDARY** — Universal Principle 3 inherited.
9. **Legal fence** — Universal Principle 5. Tax-arbitrage strategies scoped
   with tax counsel BEFORE recommendation; regulatory-arbitrage strategies
   scoped with local counsel. Sanctions / legal-restriction candidates
   blocked upstream in skill 1.
10. **§0.6 flag.** Ghemawat 2007 + Douglas & Craig + Kotler & Keller + Meyer
    2014 are Tier B. Downgrade to Tier A when Ghemawat 2007 + Meyer 2014 +
    Kotler are placed and a `Shared OS/logical/gtm_adaptation.md` Route-D
    asset is built per §8.9.

## Fallback

- **AAA-strategy unclear or contested.** Discuss trade-offs explicitly with
  operator + marcus / vista; document all 3 A's per market with explicit
  scoring; escalate final call to operator. Do NOT default to Adaptation
  without analysis.
- **PP or competitor-pricing data unavailable.** Flag "insufficient data —
  pricing recommendation deferred pending market-research." Do NOT invent
  pricing.
- **Cultural profile ambiguous** for a novel market or subculture. Consult
  Meyer + Hofstede data; if still ambiguous, note explicit uncertainty in
  messaging brief; escalate to lingua `cultural-adaptation` for deeper work.
- **Entry-mode from Phase 2 is JV or acquisition with local partner** whose
  GTM preferences conflict with recommended AAA strategy. Document conflict
  + escalate to operator + M&A / JV counsel for governance-structure resolution.
  Do NOT silently override partner's GTM preferences without governance-level
  agreement.
- **Regulatory-arbitrage or tax-arbitrage strategy candidate.** Route to
  operator + tax / regulatory counsel BEFORE recommendation. These carry
  reputational + legal risk that requires counsel-scoping.
- **Highly-regulated product category** (healthcare / financial services /
  legal services / age-gated products) — routes through canopy `entity-setup-
  by-jurisdiction` + counsel BEFORE adaptation scoping to identify regulatory
  constraints on product / pricing / channel / messaging.
- **Cross-venture GTM decision** (multiple ventures under one holding in
  same market with different GTM). Coordinate with marcus / vista for
  portfolio-level GTM strategy that upstream this skill.
- **Individual crisis signal during GTM conversation.** STOP. Route per
  Universal Principle 3 (inherited) to manager + HR Ops + EAP.
